B5 HISTORICAL COMPARISON

A original (B5): first 3/12, final 12/12, 24 attempts, 12 repairs,
  76,685 tokens, $0.042250, model 381.58 s, process 523.75 s.
B original (B5): first 0/12, final 12/12, 27 attempts, 15 repairs,
  234,217 tokens, $0.133951, model 1,243.01 s, process 1,601.24 s.
B corrected (B5): first 11/12, final 12/12, 14 attempts, 2 repairs,
  161,245 tokens, $0.092908, model 940.24 s, process 994.47 s.
C follow-up (B5): final 0/12, no timeouts; not part of B6.

Comparisons:
- B original -> B corrected changed documentation only and moved first-attempt
  success 0/12 -> 11/12, repairs 15 -> 2, tokens/cost -31%. The binary
  representation/decoder did not change. This is strong evidence that the
  documented identifier rules, not validation, explain much of B's original
  first-attempt gap.
- D did not improve raw first-attempt success: A 6/24 vs D 5/24. Post-
  generation validation cannot retroactively change the initial artifact.
- D improved total effort and final reliability: final 24/24 vs A 23/24;
  tokens -24%, cost -26%, model-step -26%, process -21%; cost per successful
  task $0.00321 vs $0.00454.
- D's validation overhead was 0.0078 s total, negligible. It also avoided B5
  compilation on invalid attempts (B5 verify time 0.302 s vs 0.750 s for A),
  but the decisive saving was lower reasoning/repair token cost.
- Per 12-task-equivalent, D cost $0.0385 and 71,444 tokens versus B corrected
  $0.0929 and 161,245 tokens; D was cheaper but B retained much higher
  historical first-attempt reliability.
- A original historical cost per successful task $0.00352; D matched cost
  $0.00321, a small pilot improvement, not statistical proof.

DIAGNOSTIC EFFECTIVENESS

D diagnostics identify location, category, expected structure and observed
structure. Examples:
  missing_top_level: expected top-level expression; observed end of file.
  invalid_control_flow: expected '[' before else block; observed ']'.
  invalid_function_reference: observed unknown word.
  argument_count: call expects N arguments; observed fewer.
For these tasks the validator caught every first-attempt structural failure
before execution. A's B5 diagnostics also prevented execution, but were
message-level rather than structured and did not expose location/category
fields. D repairs used fewer reasoning tokens, not fewer attempts.

NEW FAILURE MODES

No new failure mode was observed. The validator did not reject a final
successful D artifact. It can still reject valid Glon forms outside the
documented subset; that risk is inherent in any pre-validator and was not
exercised by these 12 tasks.

INTERPRETATION

The central hypothesis is partially supported:
- Not supported for first-attempt reliability: D raw first attempts were the
  same population as A, as expected from post-generation validation.
- Supported for total LLM effort/repair: D produced 24/24 final correct
  programs at lower token, cost and process effort than matched A, and much
  lower cost than historical B.
B's high first-attempt success is not reproduced by validation. B5's controlled
documentation correction is the strongest available explanation: changing only
the identifier documentation moved B from 0/12 to 11/12 first attempts. The
remaining role of explicit semantic roles is not separated from documentation
by B5/B6 and would require a separate prompt-level experiment, not another
representation implementation.

LIMITATIONS

- 24 matched A and D trials across two seeds; B is historical across a different
  seed/settings. This is a pilot, not statistical proof.
- Same single model/variant; no model-family replication.
- 12 small integer-subset tasks; no closures, dynamic binding, strings, GC,
  errors/judge or loader integration.
- Validation covers the documented subset; alternate valid Glon syntaxes could
  be rejected.
- B6 did not rerun B or C.
- Process time includes OpenCode startup; model-step time is reported separately.
- No reference solution was exposed; the tool-free isolation protocol was used.
